What Is Climate Change?

Weather is what is happening in the sky right now or this week — sunny, rainy, hot, or cold. Climate is the average weather pattern of a place over many years.

Climate change is a long-term shift in temperatures and weather patterns. Since the 1800s, human activities have been the main driver of climate change, mostly because we burn fossil fuels like coal, oil, and gas. Burning these fuels releases greenhouse gases that trap heat from the sun inside our atmosphere, like a blanket around the Earth. This is called the greenhouse effect, and it causes global warming — the rise in the Earth's average temperature.

Reference: adapted from NASA Climate Kids & the United Nations "What Is Climate Change" explainer.

Key vocabulary

weather
— the day-to-day condition of the atmosphere
climate
— the average weather in a place over a long time
greenhouse gas
— a gas that traps heat in the atmosphere (e.g. CO₂)
global warming
— the rise in Earth's average temperature
atmosphere
— the layer of gases surrounding the Earth
fossil fuel
— coal, oil, or gas formed from ancient plants and animals

Causes of Climate Change

  • Burning fossil fuels for electricity, factories, and vehicles releases large amounts of carbon dioxide (CO₂) into the air.
  • Deforestation — cutting down forests — means fewer trees to absorb CO₂ from the air.
  • Industrial pollution from factories adds extra greenhouse gases such as methane and nitrous oxide.
  • Transportation — cars, motorcycles, ships, and planes mostly run on fossil fuels.

Effects & Solutions

Climate change already affects our planet: sea levels are rising, storms are becoming more extreme, ice is melting, and many animals are losing their habitats. The good news — everyone can take small actions that add up to a real difference.

PROBLEM

Rising sea levels flood coastal towns
Too much CO₂ in the atmosphere
Overflowing plastic waste
High electricity use from fossil fuels

SOLUTION

Plant more trees to absorb carbon
Turn off lights and use energy wisely
Protect and restore coastal mangroves
Reduce, reuse, and recycle
